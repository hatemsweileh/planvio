# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be the kind of person other people want to build software with.

Everyone working on Planvio is here voluntarily, in their own time, across a lot of
timezones and first languages. Assume the person you are replying to is competent, busy and
acting in good faith, and you will be right almost every time.

## What that means in practice

**Do**

- Critique the code, the design or the decision. That is the whole job.
- Say *why* you disagree. "This will break workspace isolation because the policy never
  resolves membership" is useful; "this is wrong" is not.
- Assume a question is a real question. Somebody asking why the queue cannot use Redis
  usually has not read every document, and pointing them at the right one costs a sentence.
- Remember that English is a second language for many contributors. Read past the phrasing
  to the point being made.
- Accept that a maintainer may say no. Planvio has deliberate boundaries — no daemon, no
  Docker, shared hosting first — and a rejected pull request is usually about scope, not
  about you.

**Do not**

- Attack the person instead of the work. There is no version of "did you even read the
  docs" that improves a review.
- Harass anyone, publicly or privately. That includes sexualised remarks, slurs, deliberate
  misgendering, and unwelcome attention after being asked to stop.
- Publish someone's private information.
- Use an issue tracker as a place to be cruel to a stranger who wrote something imperfect.

## Scope

This applies to everything in this project's spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, code comments — and to public behaviour when you are representing Planvio.

## Enforcement

Report a problem privately to the maintainer through
[GitHub private vulnerability reporting](https://github.com/hatemsweileh/planvio/security)
or by direct message. Reports are handled confidentially, and the person who reported will
be told what happened.

Responses escalate with the behaviour:

1. **A private word.** A quiet explanation of what was wrong and why. Most things end here.
2. **A warning.** With a defined period of no interaction with the people involved.
3. **A temporary ban** from the project's spaces.
4. **A permanent ban**, for sustained harassment or aggression toward an individual.

Planvio is maintained by one person, so enforcement will not be instant. It will be fair,
and it will not depend on how much code you have contributed.
